**1. Queen Mother’s Birthday (18 June)**

* **First year as a public holiday (current era):** **1994**. Following the September 1993 restoration of the monarchy and the promulgation of the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia, the King’s and Queen’s birthdays were restored as statutory public holidays in the 1994 national calendar under Royal Government sub-decrees and Ministry of Social Affairs and Labour notices. In October 2004, following King Norodom Sihanouk’s abdication, it was officially re-designated as the Queen Mother's Birthday.
* **Pre-1970 status:** **No.** During the Sangkum Reastr Niyum era (1955–1970), the official royal holidays commemorated King Norodom Suramarit and Queen Sisowath Kossamak. Queen Monineath (then Princess Monique) was not the reigning Queen consort, and her birthday was not an official state holiday under the 1947 Constitution or pre-1970 royal decrees.

**2. National Environmental Sanitation Day (23 November)**

* **Founding instrument & first year:** **Sub-decree No. 63 ANKr.BK of 23 November 1995** (*អនុក្រឹត្យលេខ ៦៣ អនក្រ.បក ចុះថ្ងៃទី ២៣ ខែវិច្ឆិកា ឆ្នាំ១៩៩៥*).
* **Verification:** Sub-decree 63 exists and was co-signed on 23 November 1995 by First Prime Minister Norodom Ranariddh and Second Prime Minister Hun Sen, officially establishing 23 November as National Environmental Sanitation Day (*ទិវាអនាម័យបរិស្ថានជាតិ*). The first observance was conducted on **23 November 1995** to launch the national cleanup initiative. Claims citing 2017 refer to an updated Ministry of Environment campaign reform, not the founding instrument.

**3. International Buddhist Day (8 April)**

* **Exact observance dates:**
* **2022:** Marked on **8 April 2022** at Svay Pope Pagoda and national Buddhist institutions, presided over by Supreme Patriarch Bour Kry and Ministry of Cults and Religion officials.
* **2023:** Marked on **8 April 2023**.


* **Founding instrument:** Cambodia formally adopted the day in solidarity with the World Buddhist Summit's international declaration (8 April, traditional East Asian Vesak/Hanamatsuri). The Royal Government formally codified its permanent inclusion on the national observance register via **Sub-decree of 16 August 2024** (*អនុក្រឹត្យ ស្តីពីការកំណត់យកថ្ងៃទី ៨ ខែមេសា ជាទិវាព្រះពុទ្ធសាសនាអន្តរជាតិ*).

**5. Cambodia National Mine Awareness Day (24 February)**

* **Founding instrument & first year:** Established by Royal Government Decision in late 1999 under Prime Minister Hun Sen; the first nationwide observance was held on **24 February 2000**. Official Cambodian commemorative counts confirm this origin (e.g., the 25th National Mine Awareness Day was observed on 24 February 2024).
* **What the date marks:** Commemorates King Norodom Sihanouk’s national appeal against anti-personnel mines issued in late February, as well as the preparatory integration of national demining policy that culminated in the 1999 Law on the Prohibition of the Use of Anti-Personnel Mines. Claims attributing the formal institutional day to a 1995 Sihanouk decree confuse royal anti-mine public statements made in 1994–1995 with the government’s 1999 executive establishment.

**6. Cambodian Day for Older Persons (1 October)**

* **Base year & first national observance:** **1999** (the UN International Year of Older Persons).
* **Anniversary count:** The official Cambodian calendar count begins with 1999 as the 1st national observance (*ទិវាមនុស្សចាស់កម្ពុជា និងទិវាមនុស្សចាស់អន្តរជាតិ*), coordinated by the Ministry of Social Affairs, Veterans and Youth Rehabilitation. Consequently:
* 2023 = 25th observance
* 2024 = 26th observance
* 2026 = **28th observance**


* **The 1994/1999 discrepancy:** The "sixth annual" report in 1999 reflects local NGO programs (predominantly HelpAge International Cambodia) that began marking the UN's 1990 resolution on a grassroots level in 1994, prior to formal state institutionalization in 1999.

**7. Water Policy Day (4 March)**

* **Event commemorated:** The formal adoption of the **National Water Resources Policy of the Kingdom of Cambodia** (*គោលនយោបាយជាតិស្តីពីការគ្រប់គ្រងធនធានទឹក*) by the Council of Ministers on **4 March 2000**. The commemorative formulation ("4 March 2000 – 4 March 2026") marks the adoption of this foundational policy instrument, which later served as the blueprint for the 2007 Law on Water Resources Management.

**8. Win-Win Policy Commemorations (29 December: 1999–2021)**

* **1999–2017:** Celebrated annually by the Ministry of National Defence, the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces (RCAF), and the Cambodian People's Party (CPP) as a military-political victory mark rather than a statutory holiday. Major milestone parades took place on the 5th (2003), 10th (2008), and 15th (2013) anniversaries.
* **2018:** Marked with a three-day national state celebration (29–31 December 2018) for the 20th anniversary and the inauguration of the Win-Win Monument (*វិមានឈ្នះ-ឈ្នះ*).
* **2019–2021:** Formalized across all civilian state institutions via **Government Circular No. 06 SR (25 February 2019)**, directing all ministries to mark 29 December annually as "Peace Day and the Day of Win-Win Policy Implementation" without granting a work-free day, before its eventual elevation to a paid public holiday in 2024.

**9. King-Father Commemoration Decree (22 October 2012)**

* **Sub-decree number:** **Sub-decree No. 186 ANKr.BK** (*អនុក្រឹត្យលេខ ១៨៦ អនក្រ.បក ចុះថ្ងៃទី ២២ ខែតុលា ឆ្នាំ២០១២*).
* **Verification:** Sub-decree 186 officially designated 15 October as an annual national paid public holiday in honor of King Father Norodom Sihanouk. Sub-decree No. 191 ANKr.BK was issued later (25 October 2012) and pertained to the formation and logistical mandate of the National Commission for Organizing the Royal Funeral.

**10. National Commemorations: First Observance Years**

* **Teachers' Day (5 October):** **1997**. Established by the Royal Government under Hun Sen to align with World Teachers’ Day (first adopted globally by UNESCO in 1994); Cambodia’s first national celebration was held on **5 October 1997**.
* **Indigenous Peoples' Day (9 August):** **2005**. First officially celebrated nationwide on **9 August 2005**, co-organized by the Ministry of Rural Development, the UN, and the Cambodia Indigenous Peoples Organization at the launch of the UN Second International Decade of the World's Indigenous People.
* **Persons with Disabilities Day (3 December):** **1999**. Formally established under Ministry of Social Affairs guidelines in 1999; the 1st Cambodian Day of Persons with Disabilities was observed alongside the UN International Day on **3 December 1999**.

**11. Khmer Spelling & 1984 Official Name for "Day of Hatred" (20 May)**

* **Spelling check:** **ទិវាចងកំហឹង** is the exact and standard Khmer spelling used during the People’s Republic of Kampuchea (PRK) era.
* **Official 1984 title:** When instituted by resolution of the PRK National Assembly in 1983 and first observed nationally on 20 May 1984, its full official designation was **ទិវាចងកំហឹងចំពោះរបបប្រល័យពូជសាសន៍ ប៉ុល ពត អៀង សារី ខៀវ សំផន** (*Day of Harboring Anger against the Genocidal Pol Pot-Ieng Sary-Khieu Samphan Regime*), often shortened in official media (such as *Kampuchea* newspaper) to **ទិវាចងកំហឹង ២០ ឧសភា** (*Day of Hatred, 20 May*). In 2018, Sub-decree No. 19 ANKr.BK officially renamed the holiday to **ទិវាជាតិនៃការចងចាំ** (*National Day of Remembrance*).
