# Khmer calendar start years and counting conventions

**The King-Father fix to 2013 is supported. The Coronation Day fix to 2004 is also supported as the original occasion, but 2005 was the first annual anniversary holiday.** A 2014 start is incorrect for either event. ([The Cambodia Daily][1])

Throughout this brief, **first observance**, **legal designation**, **public-holiday status**, and **anniversaryBase** are treated separately. “International fallback” means that I established the international start but did **not** establish Cambodia’s first participation. An unknown Cambodian first year should not be replaced by an earliest-attested year without labeling that distinction.

## Priority 0 — the two applied fixes

### 1. King’s Coronation Day — 29 October

**Cambodia: original occasion and public holiday, 2004-10-29; first annual anniversary holiday, 2005-10-29; `anniversaryBase = 2004`.**

Contemporary reporting dated **26 October 2004** describes the royal coronation programme for **28–30 October 2004**, with all three days declared public holidays. The coronation itself was on **29 October**. Thus, 2004 is not merely a retrospectively assigned historical base: the original occasion was officially observed as a holiday. ([The Cambodia Daily][1])

A **2 March 2005** report identifies a sub-decree signed on **10 February 2005** that added **29 October, the coronation anniversary**, to the holiday calendar. This establishes the recurring anniversary holiday well before 2014. The amended **2013** holiday schedule also explicitly includes 29 October. ([The Cambodia Daily][2])

**Catalog decision:** keep **2004** when the record includes the original coronation; use **2005** only for a field explicitly meaning “first annual anniversary.” I did not retrieve every annual decree for **2006–2012**, so this is not an exhaustive year-by-year certification of the entire 2004–2013 sequence.

### 2. King-Father Commemoration Day — 15 October

**Cambodia: first annual observance, 2013-10-15; `anniversaryBase = 2012`.**

The identifiable legal reference is **Sub-decree No. 186, dated 22 October 2012**, amending Article 1 of **Sub-decree No. 156** on paid holidays for **2013**. Its schedule expressly includes **15 October, commemoration of King Norodom Sihanouk**. DFDL’s contemporary November 2012 legal update reproduces the amended schedule; I did not retrieve the original signed decree scan. **2013, not 2014, is therefore supported.** ([DFDL][3])

**There was official action on 2012-10-15 itself, but it was the response to his death, not an annual anniversary observance.** The King’s official website records King Sihamoni and Prime Minister Hun Sen leaving for Beijing that morning to escort the King-Father’s body home. Contemporary reporting also identifies a mourning directive signed on **15 October**, with the national mourning period beginning **17 October**. ([Norodom Sihamoni][4])

### 16. International Day of Peace — fixed date versus earlier rule

**International: first observed in 1982; fixed 21 September begins in 2002.**

The essential correction is confirmed: **UNGA resolution 55/282, adopted 7 September 2001, makes 21 September the annual date beginning in 2002**. The original **resolution 36/67, adopted 30 November 1981**, linked the observance to the **opening day of the General Assembly’s regular session**. The first actual observance was in **1982**, not 1981. ([UN Press][18])

However, **“third Tuesday of September, 1981–2001” is not a safe exact historical implementation**. It is a common summary of the former arrangement, but the operative rule concerned the session’s opening, and exceptions occurred. The UN’s history records that the **2001 peace observance was postponed after the 11 September attacks and marked on 14 September**. ([United Nations][19])

**Recommended modeling:** no occurrence in 1981; a historical date table or session-opening rule with overrides for **1982–2001**; fixed **09-21 from 2002**.

### 17. National Day of Remembrance — 20 May

**Cambodia: original “Day of Hatred” observance, 1984-05-20; formal modern National Day of Remembrance/public-holiday designation, 2018.**

The historical observance began in **1984**. The modern national-holiday designation under the remembrance name dates to **2018**, associated with **Sub-decree No. 19**. These should be separate metadata milestones. ([Auschwitz Institute][20])

**Do not model this as an uninterrupted, identically named annual observance from 1984.** I did not establish a sufficiently documented single year for the intervening local revival or the first informal use of the present name. Accordingly, **1984 is the historical origin and 2018 the verified modern statutory milestone**, not necessarily the beginning of every activity now called remembrance.

## Priority 2 — Cambodia-specific origins

### 18. Queen Mother’s birthday — 18 June

**Cambodia: exact first official observance in the current era remains unresolved.**

The amended government holiday schedule for **2013** expressly includes **18 June, the Queen’s birthday**, so official public-holiday status is established **by 2013-06-18**. This does **not** prove that 2013 was the first year. ([DFDL][3])

Do not use **1936**, her birth year, as the first-observance year, nor assume that restoration of the monarchy automatically establishes the first birthday holiday. For now, retain an unknown first year and a separately labeled **earliest verified holiday attestation: 2013**.

### 19. Arbor Day — រុក្ខទិវា, 9 July

**Cambodia: origin 1952; modern statutory annual date, 9 July, reinforced in 2002.**

FAO’s Cambodia account dates the tradition to **1952**, under King Norodom Sihanouk, and identifies the **2002 Forestry Law** as establishing the annual 9 July observance. ([FAOHome][21])

The historical sequence was interrupted: reporting identifies a revival in **1990** and annual observance from **1992**. Thus **1952 is the origin**, but should not be interpreted as proof of continuous annual ceremonies through every subsequent political period. ([Cambodianess][22])

### 20. National Population Day — 11 July

**Cambodia: 2026-07-11 is the first year/date under the newly designated national day.**

This has a very recent legal origin. A sub-decree dated **21 May 2026**, publicized on **22 May**, designated **11 July annually as National Population Day**, with the Ministry of Planning responsible for organizing it. ([https://akp.gov.kh][23])

**Do not backdate this national designation to earlier World Population Day activities.** Those are a related international observance, not evidence that the newly designated Cambodian national day existed under the same legal identity.

### 21. Water Policy Day — ទិវានយោបាយទឹក, 4 March

**Cambodia: documented count-from/origin reference, 2000-03-04; exact first annual ceremony not independently established.**

The published **2026** message explicitly describes the **26th anniversary** and supplies the date range **4 March 2000–4 March 2026**. This supports **`anniversaryBase = 2000`** directly. ([Fresh News][24])

It does not, by itself, prove whether an inaugural observance occurred in 2000 or the first anniversary ceremony in 2001. I also did not retrieve the founding policy instrument establishing exactly which decision or announcement on **4 March 2000** the date commemorates. Record **2000 as the supported origin/base**, with the first-observance field qualified rather than silently inferred.

### 22. National Mine Awareness Day — 24 February

**Cambodia: `anniversaryBase = 1999` is supported; “first observed in 2000” is not established.**

A joint **CMAA–UNDP** release calls the **2024** event the **25th anniversary**; government reporting calls the **2026** event the **27th**. Both support the arithmetic base **1999**. ([UNDP][25])

The origin evidence is contradictory. A **2021 Phnom Penh Post** report says the day has been observed since **1999**. But a contemporary **2010** report quotes a senior Cambodian Mine Action Authority official saying that King Sihanouk established the **24 February** date in **1995**. ([Open Development Mekong][26])

**Catalog decision:** retain **base 1999**, but do **not** claim that arithmetic proves first observance in 2000. The exact inaugural year and the historical reason for choosing **24 February** remain unresolved pending the founding instrument. The day’s documented purpose is mine-risk awareness, especially for communities living in contaminated areas. ([Open Development Mekong][26])

### 23. Cambodian Older Persons Day — 1 October

**International first observance: 1991-10-01. Cambodia: an official observance on 1998-10-01 is documented, but not established as the first.**

A contemporary report dated **2 October 1998** describes the previous day’s Cambodian observance, including a prime-ministerial statement and activities involving the ministry and UNDP. Thus, **something official did occur on 1 October 1998**. ([The Cambodia Daily][27])

That does not make 1998 the origin. A separate **1999** report describes a **sixth annual tribute to older people**, indicating an earlier Cambodian history, although it does not establish a clean sequence of 1 October observances. ([The Cambodia Daily][28])

Use **1991** only as an explicitly **international fallback**; leave Cambodia’s exact first year unresolved. The UN designation was made through **resolution 45/106 on 14 December 1990**, followed by the first international observance on **1 October 1991**. ([United Nations][29])

### 24. National and World Environment Day — 5 June

**Three different years belong in the record: international first observance, 1973; Cambodian participation in World Environment Day, 1993; Cambodian National Environment Day designation, 1996.**

UNEP explicitly states that World Environment Day has been held annually **since 1973**. Therefore **1974 should not be used as the global first-observance year** on the strength of commonly repeated secondary accounts. ([UNEP - UN Environment Programme][30])

Reporting on Cambodia’s royal observance identifies **1993** as Cambodia’s start in celebrating World Environment Day, and **1996** as the creation of **National Environment Day by sub-decree**, also on 5 June. I found this account, but not the original 1996 decree scan or number. ([Cambodianess][31])

**Catalog decision:** **1996 is a real Cambodian national-origin claim**, not merely an arbitrary default. Keep the national and international histories distinct. None of these first-observance dates alone establishes the anniversary number that a particular calendar prints.

### 25. Win-Win Policy Day versus Peace Day — 29 December

**Cambodia: historical base 1998-12-29; formal national-calendar inclusion documented in 2022; Peace Day public holiday begins 2024-12-29.**

The **1 January 2024 sub-decree** designated **29 December as Cambodia’s Peace Day and a public holiday**, commemorating **29 December 1998**. The corresponding labour holiday implementation followed in **Prakas No. 1 of 5 January 2024**. Thus **2024 is supported as the first year of the new statutory Peace Day holiday**. ([Cambodianess][32])

The same contemporary account says that the government had added **29 December to the national calendar in January 2022**, but it was **not then a public holiday**. ([Cambodianess][32])

Consequently, I cannot confirm an officially designated, uninterrupted annual **“Win-Win Policy Day” for every year 1999–2023**. Do not infer that sequence merely from the **1998** anniversary base. Model the historical event, earlier commemorations, **2022 calendar designation**, and **2024 holiday/name designation** separately.

### 26. Anti-Human Trafficking Day — 12 December

**Cambodia: 2007-12-12; government recognition dated 2007-12-07.**

This has a strong government reference. Cambodia’s report to the UN Committee on the Rights of the Child, **CRC/C/KHM/2–3, paragraph 241**, states that **Notice No. 1748, dated 7 December 2007**, recognized **12 December as Anti-Human Trafficking Day**. This supports **2007**, not 2006, for the national observance. ([docstore.ohchr.org][33])

**International Buddhist Day — 8 April requires a separate explanation.**

**2024 is Cambodia’s legal-designation year, not its first known celebration.** The Cambodian sub-decree is dated **16 August 2024**, after that year’s 8 April. Therefore **8 April 2025** is the first annual date following that instrument. ([IBCC Cambodia][38])

But Cambodia’s Ministry of Cults and Religions was already issuing instructions for the day, and provincial authorities were celebrating it, in **April 2022**. Thus, **neither 2024 nor 2025 is defensible as the first-ever Cambodian observance**; the exact Cambodian inaugural year remains unestablished. ([Phnom Penh Post][39])

The observance was established internationally at the **Sixth Buddhist Summit in Kobe in 2014**, by Buddhist leaders from 41 countries, using **8 April** as the Buddha-birthday observance. It is **not the UN’s International Day of Vesak**. The latter was recognized through **UNGA resolution 54/115 of 1999** and follows the movable Vesak/full-moon observance rather than a fixed 8 April. ([全世界仏教サミット][40])

### 28. International Anti-Corruption Day — 9 December

**International first observance: 2004-12-09.**

The contemporary UN Secretary-General’s briefing for **8 December 2004** explicitly identifies the following day as the **first International Anti-Corruption Day**. That is stronger evidence for first observance than simply taking the year of **resolution 58/4, adopted 31 October 2003**. ([United Nations][41])

**Use 2004**, with **2003 retained as the designation/UNCAC-adoption year**. Cambodia’s own inaugural observance was not established. Transparency International’s 2004 annual report independently corroborates the first observance in December 2004. ([Transparency.org][42])

### 34. World Water Day and World Meteorological Day

**The official international dates are separate: World Water Day on 22 March, and World Meteorological Day on 23 March. Split the underlying event records.**

WMO explicitly distinguishes these dates, including in years when the two observances shared a theme and were intended to have coordinated celebrations. A joint ceremony is not, by itself, a change to either observance’s calendar date. ([World Meteorological Organization][49])

I did not establish a Cambodian legal instrument creating a permanently merged **22 March** observance. Therefore the defensible implementation is **two dated records**, with an optional combined display label or separately dated joint ceremony where a Cambodian source documents one.

## Recommended catalog treatment

Keep `firstObservanceYear` and `anniversaryBase` independent, and add a scope such as **Cambodia** or **international fallback**. Where necessary, retain separate fields for `establishedOn`, `publicHolidayFrom`, `earliestAttestedYear`, and date-rule periods. An anniversary number can establish an arithmetic base without establishing the first celebration.

The principal unresolved items are the Queen Mother’s exact inaugural holiday year, Environmental Sanitation Day’s proposed 2017 origin, Mine Awareness Day’s conflicting origins and date significance, the first Cambodian Older Persons Day and Buddhist Day, and Water Policy Day’s founding instrument. The evidence also does not yet certify every Coronation Day decree from 2006–2012 or the exact typography/counts in an identified LRC printed-calendar edition. Those gaps should remain explicit rather than be filled with inferred “first” years.
