## Findings

The strongest corrections are **Sub-decree No. 47 of 12 June 1995** for National Environmental Sanitation Day, and **No. 186 of 22 October 2012** for the amendment adding the King-Father commemoration. The latter is identified in a contemporary legal update; I did not obtain its original Khmer scan. ([FAOLEX][1])

“Unknown” below means that the requested fact remains unverified—not that the holiday or observance did not exist.

### 1. Queen Mother’s birthday — 18 June

**First official public-holiday year in the current era: Unknown. The proposed 1994 start is not verified.**

The firm documentary attestation obtained is the **2013 public-holiday list**, as amended by **Sub-decree No. 186, dated 22 October 2012**. DFDL’s legal update of **22 November 2012** lists **18 June** as the Queen Mother’s birthday holiday. This establishes holiday status for **18 June 2013**, not its first year. ([DFDL][2])

The 1994 cutoff appears in a software holiday implementation, but that is not a founding instrument or contemporary holiday list and should not be used to establish the historical start. ([Dgit Debian][3])

**Holiday status under a pre-1970 government: Unknown.** I did not locate a contemporary official holiday list or dated report establishing that Monineath’s **18 June** birthday was then a public holiday.

### 2. National Environmental Sanitation Day — 23 November

**Legal establishment: 12 June 1995, by Sub-decree No. 47.**

The English text retained by **FAOLEX** is titled *Sub-Decree No. 47 on the Creation of the National Clean Environment Day*. Its provisions establish the day, prescribe its celebration on **23 November every year**, and make the instrument effective from signature. It is dated **12 June 1995** and signed by the two prime ministers, Norodom Ranariddh and Hun Sen. ([FAOLEX][1])

This identification is independently corroborated by the **UNEP/FAOLEX catalogue** and by a **23 November 2019 Fresh News report** quoting Environment Ministry spokesman Neth Pheaktra on the **12 June 1995** decree.  ([freshnews.com.kh][4])

**Disposition of the competing claims:**
The citation **“No. 63 ANKr.BK, 23 November 1995” is not verified as this day’s founding instrument**. The verified instrument is **No. 47, 12 June 1995**. A **2017 establishment** is incompatible with that earlier legal text. **23 November 1995** was the first annual date prescribed after the decree, but I did not obtain a report proving that the inaugural ceremony actually occurred that day. ([FAOLEX][1])

### 3. International Buddhist Day — Cambodia

**First Cambodian observance: Unknown.**

The **Phnom Penh Post, 11 April 2022**, article *“King Sihamoni and PM lauded on International Buddhist Day”* establishes Cambodian ministerial recognition in **2022**. It identifies the recurring day as **8 April** and discusses that year’s dedication by the Ministry of Cults and Religions. It does **not** identify 2022 as Cambodia’s first observance.

| Requested fact                                   | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Exact Cambodian ceremony/observance date in 2022 | **Unknown.** The report identifies the annual date as **8 April**, but the text retrieved does not establish a separately dated Cambodian ceremony.                                                                                                                          |
| Exact Cambodian ceremony/observance date in 2023 | **Unknown.** No sufficiently specific dated record was obtained.                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Formal government designation                    | **16 August 2024** sub-decree, designating **8 April** and assigning annual organization to the Ministry of Cults and Religions. The decree’s date and provisions are reported by the International Business Chamber of Cambodia; its number was not independently verified. |

The 2024 designation therefore must not be substituted for the first Cambodian observance year. ([IBCC Cambodia][5])

### 4. International Day of Peace — 1982–2000

The governing distinction is confirmed by the UN: **Resolution 36/67 of 30 November 1981** tied the day to the regular session’s opening; **Resolution 52/232 of 4 June 1998** reaffirmed that arrangement. The fixed **21 September** date took effect in **2002**, under **Resolution 55/282 of 7 September 2001**. ([UN Press][6])

### 5. Cambodia National Mine Awareness Day — 24 February

**Founding instrument: Unknown. Historical event commemorated by the choice of 24 February: Unknown.**

The **1995 royal-establishment claim has an identifiable dated source**: *The Cambodia Daily*, **25 February 2010**, “Mine Awareness Day Brought To Cambodia’s Far Corners.” It quotes **Leng Sochea**, permanent deputy secretary-general of the Cambodian Mine Action and Victim Assistance Authority, attributing the establishment of **24 February** to **King Norodom Sihanouk in 1995**. The report does not supply the royal instrument’s number or date. ([The Cambodia Daily][22])

The competing chronology is reflected in official anniversary usage: the **UNDP/CMAA joint release of 22 February 2024** calls the event the **25th anniversary** of National Mine Awareness Day. That count is evidence of official anniversary usage, but it does not itself establish a **1999 Hun Sen declaration**, nor a **first observance in 2000**. ([UNDP][23])

**Result:** the royal-1995 attribution is documented, but the conflict is **not resolved at founding-instrument level**. Neither the alleged 1999 declaration nor what historical occurrence the date commemorates was verified.

### 6. Cambodian day for older persons — 1 October

**First Cambodian observance year: Unknown. An observance on 1 October 1998 is verified.**

*The Cambodia Daily*, **2 October 1998**, “King Honored on Elderly Day Aimed to Spur Return to Year Zero,” reports the Ministry of Social Affairs/UNDP celebration held the preceding Thursday, **1 October 1998**. It does not call this the first celebration. ([The Cambodia Daily][24])

The potentially earlier chronology comes from *The Cambodia Daily*, **5 September 1999**, “Elderly Bemoan Coldness, Corruption of New Generation.” It describes the country’s **“sixth annual tribute to the elderly”**, presided over by the Queen. However, the report concerns an elderly tribute and International Year of Older Persons activities; it does not establish that this was the same annual **1 October Cambodian Day**. **1994 should not be assigned as the founding year from that phrase alone.** ([The Cambodia Daily][25])

**Printed anniversary base / “2026 = 29th”: Unknown.** A relevant contrary count is documented: **CamboJA, 7 November 2024**, reporting the government’s **27 August 2024 circular**, calls the **1 October 2024** event the **26th Cambodian Day of Older Persons and International Day of Older Persons**. I did not verify a 2026 calendar printing “29th,” or a source explicitly naming the intended base year. ([CamboJA News][26])

### 7. Water Policy Day — ទិវានយោបាយទឹក, 4 March

**Explicit commemorative base date: 4 March 2000. Specific decision or event on that date: Unknown.**

**Fresh News, 4 March 2026, 07:36**, reports Hun Sen’s message celebrating the **26th anniversary**, explicitly printing **“4 March 2000–4 March 2026.”** Thus the 2000 base date is directly stated, not reconstructed from the anniversary count. ([Fresh News][27])

The article describes the day’s water-management and agricultural-irrigation purposes, but does **not** name a **4 March 2000 decree, policy adoption, speech, conference, or inauguration**. The underlying 2000 event remains unidentified. ([Fresh News][27])

### 8. Win-Win Policy commemorations — 29 December, before 2022

**Official commemorative activity before 2022 is verified. An uninterrupted annual series beginning in 1999 is not.**

**1999–2017: Unknown in this search.** Later anniversary counts do not establish that official annual commemorations took place in every intervening year.

### 9. King-Father commemoration decree — 186 or 191?

**The documented number is No. 186, dated 22 October 2012—not No. 191.**

DFDL’s **22 November 2012** legal update identifies **Sub-decree No. 186**, amending Article 1 of **Sub-decree No. 156** concerning the 2013 holidays. The amended list includes **15 October**, the commemoration of King Norodom Sihanouk, alongside the restored **23 October** Paris Peace Agreements holiday. ([DFDL][2])

**Source qualification:** this is a contemporary legal report identifying the instrument, not an inspected original Khmer decree. I found no substantiated No. 191 citation for this amendment.

### 11. Khmer spelling and the official 1984 “Day of Hatred” name

**Yes: ទិវាចងកំហឹង is a documented Khmer name for the PRK-era day.**

A **TVK report dated 20 May 2026**, quoting an official commemorative address, uses exactly **«ទិវាចងកំហឹង»**. It states that the PRK National Assembly adopted **20 May** as that day on **18 August 1983**, with annual observance beginning in **1984**. This is an official retrospective account, not the original 1983 resolution. ([tvk.gov.kh][35])

The near-contemporary **U.S. government country study** records the expanded English designation as:

> Day of Hatred “against the genocidal Pol Pot-Ieng Sary-Khieu Samphan clique and the Sihanouk-Son Sann reactionary groups.”

It identifies the observance as **20 May**. Notably, this wording does **not** insert “King” before Sihanouk. ([Country Studies][36])

**Exact full official Khmer title in the original 1984 programme or founding resolution: Unknown.** The short Khmer name is verified; the longer English political designation is documented in the country study, but I cannot certify it as a verbatim translation of an inspected 1984 PRK instrument.
